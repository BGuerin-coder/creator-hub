# Databases

## Status

Proposed - 2026/10/06

## Context

Transactions and donations are sensitive and must be isolated from public content, so that a flaw in the public zone does not expose payment data (see [ADR 0003](./0003_public_sensitive_zones.md)).

## Decision

Two separate databases: one for public content, and one for transactions and donations, read only by the sensitive service. The database engine (probably PostgreSQL) is still to be confirmed.

## Consequences

### Benefits

- The transactions are only read by one service.
- A compromise of the public database does not expose transactions.

### Cons

- Two databases to back up, migrate and monitor.

## Alternatives considered

- One database with separate schemas and access roles per zone, to be evaluated.

## Open questions

- Do we need our own transactions database at all, or could the payment provider's records be the source of truth (same question as in 0003 and 0004)?
- Is PostgreSQL confirmed, and is it self-hosted in Docker or a managed service?
- What is the backup strategy and frequency, especially for the sensitive database?
- How are migrations handled across two databases for a single developer (tooling, ORM)?
- Where does personal data about donors and customers live, and what is the retention / deletion policy (GDPR right to erasure)?

## Revisit triggers

- If operating two databases becomes too costly for one developer, one database with strict roles could be considered.
